# What Not to Buy: High-Risk Signals on Micro App Listings

By **Joshua Chen** · [About / experience]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link about.md %}) · urbanu619@gmail.com


Most bad acquisitions fail the same way: the screenshot looked fine, the transfer did not, and the revenue depended on a rule the platform can change overnight.

Use this as a **kill list** before deep diligence. Pair it with the [full checklist]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link due-diligence-checklist.md %}). One hard fail is enough to walk or reprice as scrap.

---

## Category kills (usually do not buy in round one)

### 1. Businesses that exist only while a platform looks away

Patterns:

- Unofficial story / media downloaders
- Unauthorized IPTV or content bypass tools
- Scrapers and "analytics" that violate a host platform's ToS
- Automation glued to a single social API with no contractual access

They can show short-term cash. Policy change zeroes the asset. Distribution here is rented without a lease.

### 2. Famous IP without a transferable license

Names, characters, art, music, sports marks, movie skins — if the seller cannot show:

- Trademark permission
- Asset licenses
- **Transferability** of that permission to you
- A clean store complaint history

…you are buying a takedown timer.

### 3. Generic AI shells with no unique data or distribution

Common skins: AI diary, calorie, resume, writing, image, dating assistant — with no proprietary dataset, workflow, or owned channel.

Easy to copy, expensive to acquire users, low switching costs. Price as a weekend prototype, not as a SaaS.

### 4. "Dev complete" with zero proof of use

UI, source zip, and marketing screenshots are **software assets**. They are not companies. Compare to rebuild cost ([codebase takeover costs]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link codebase-takeover-cost.md %})); never apply revenue-multiple thinking ([asset types]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link asset-types.md %})).

### 5. Gray revenue and fuzzy compliance

Do not let a Stripe graph override:

- Copyright
- Privacy and consent
- Right to transfer customer data
- Payments and refunds exposure
- Medical / financial claims
- Age gates
- Store policy history

If compliance is unclear, the downside is not a bad month — it is account loss.

## Listing-behavior red flags

Freeze the deal until you have evidence — or leave.

| Signal | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| "No time" **and** every metric falling | Exit narrative may hide a dying product |
| Huge traffic, tiny revenue, no coherent story | Bot traffic, junk SEO, or broken monetization |
| Screenshots disagree with read-only dashboards | You cannot underwrite fiction |
| Seller blocks read-only verification | No proof, no deal |
| One customer is most of revenue | You are buying a contract, not a product |
| Near-100% churn or extreme refunds | Subscription model is cosplay |
| Critical API or publisher account cannot transfer | You bought a demo |
| Growth dies without the seller's personal brand | Job, not asset |
| No version history | Forensic and rebuild risk |
| Spike-then-list (revenue juiced into the listing) | Window-dressed trailing twelve |
| Store already warned or dinged the app | Transfer may inherit the problem |
| Depends on scraping or bypassing platform rules | Policy risk is the product |

## Soft caution (buy only with a fix you own)

These are not automatic kills if **you** have a concrete 90-day repair:

- Strong distribution, weak paywall — and you know how to price that niche
- One platform (iOS only) — and you will ship the other
- English-only — and you have a real localization plan into a market you understand
- Tool without content/SEO — and content is your channel

The test: write the fix in one paragraph. If you cannot, you are hoping, not buying.

---

## Fast filter before you open the data room

1. Can the store listing, domain, and payment rails transfer under official rules?
2. Is revenue verified read-only, with refunds and concentration visible?
3. Does the product die if one platform changes a ToS clause?
4. Is IP and data transfer documented, not assumed?
5. Would you still want the zip at rebuild cost if users and ranking vanished tomorrow?

Two or more "no" → do not spend diligence hours. Build or hire instead.
