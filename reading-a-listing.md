# How to Read a Listing Without Believing the Screenshot

By **Joshua Chen** · [About / experience](about.md) · urbanu619@gmail.com


Marketplace pages are sales pages. The job is not to memorize six famous deals — it is to recognize **patterns** that keep reappearing.

This is a method demo. No named listings, no exact ask prices, no seller-reported profit figures. For kill signals in checklist form, see [what not to buy](what-not-to-buy.md). For type mix-ups, see [three asset types](asset-types.md).

---

## Pattern 1 — Boring reminder tool, busy page, thin wallet

**What it looks like:** Document / license / renewal reminders. “Dull but forever” job. Listing page gets a lot of views. Monetization looks small relative to attention.

**How to read it**

- Boredom is a feature: recurring admin pain can be a real Type A wedge.
- High attention + low revenue is not automatically “upside.” It often means wrong price, wrong traffic, or tire-kickers.
- Expand mentally to family, SMB, domains, permits — only after you verify **paying** users and renewal shape.

**Diligence focus:** paid user count, renewal mix, traffic source quality — not page-view vanity.

---

## Pattern 2 — Ultra-vertical utility with a big-platform plug-in

**What it looks like:** Narrow hobby or sport log (maintenance, sessions, gear). Some organic installs and store reviews. Integrates with a large third-party graph (fitness, maps, marketplaces).

**How to read it**

- Vertical focus can mean low competition and clear keywords.
- The integration that makes the demo sing is often the transfer and policy risk.
- Upside stories (parts life, shop referrals, affiliate) are optional chapters — not in the purchase price until proven.

**Diligence focus:** subscription migration, official store transfer, whether the third-party token is even assignable.

---

## Pattern 3 — “Cheap” multiple on non-trivial revenue

**What it looks like:** Trailing revenue that is not tiny, yet ask price sits near or under about one year of that revenue (or otherwise looks like a fire sale vs. common micro bands).

**How to read it**

- Low multiple is a **signal**, not a coupon. Sellers discount for a reason: scrape risk, API fragility, AI COGS, concentration, or a cliff you have not seen.
- Split “revenue” into profit, annual prepay cliffs, refunds, and trend before you celebrate.

**Diligence focus:** data source legality, API/cost stack, cohort trend. Then re-read [why multiples compress](valuation-multiples.md).

---

## Pattern 4 — Generic AI shell with some installs

**What it looks like:** Image/video/writing wrapper. Store presence and a bit of profit. Category is loud and crowded.

**How to read it**

- Listing + organic installs are Type B ingredients — real, but not a moat.
- Model bills, paid UA, and clone speed eat margin.
- Without owned distribution or a ruthless vertical, you are renting a demo.

**Diligence focus:** unit economics after model cost, differentiation beyond the model brand, what remains if the API price doubles.

---

## Pattern 5 — Content database sold as a subscription

**What it looks like:** Directory / map / funding list / “research hub.” Decent browsing interest, modest profit, and a churn number that looks absurd (including near-total loss of subscribers in a period).

**How to read it**

- The content pile may be useful; the **billing model** may be wrong (one-time research vs. monthly habit).
- Extreme churn is an alarm, not a rounding error.
- Rescue ideas (deadline radar, continuous refresh) belong in your 90-day plan — price the current machine, not the fantasy.

**Diligence focus:** cohort retention, why people paid once, whether data refresh is operationally affordable.

---

## Pattern 6 — Store shell, almost no revenue

**What it looks like:** Low four-figure (or similar) ask. App exists. Revenue is negligible. Pitch leans on “built,” “published,” or hours invested.

**How to read it**

- This is Type C: source + listing experience + time — not a business.
- Underwrite with rebuild cost and transfer friction only.
- Never import ARR-multiple language.

**Diligence focus:** code quality, license cleanliness, whether the store entry can move. See [codebase takeover costs](codebase-takeover-cost.md).

## One worksheet for any listing

Copy this into your notes before you message the seller:

```text
Pattern match (1–6 or other):
Asset type (A / B / C):
What the screenshot wants me to feel:
What would make this pattern a trap:
Evidence I will demand read-only:
90-day fix I personally can execute:
Walk-away line:
```

If you cannot fill “trap” and “evidence,” you are browsing, not buying.
