# Why Micro-SaaS Often Sells at “Only” 2–4× ARR

By **Joshua Chen** · [About / experience](about.md) · urbanu619@gmail.com


A house can clear twenty-plus years of rent. A micro-SaaS clearing two to four years of recurring revenue looks “cheap” until you price what can vanish overnight.

This page explains the **compression**, not a price oracle. Use it after you classify the asset ([three types](asset-types.md)).

---

## The planning band (not a promise)

Across public buyer/seller guides checked on **2026-09-30**, micro and small private SaaS deals are commonly discussed in roughly:

| Lens | Band often cited for small / micro deals | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ARR multiple | about **2×–4×** (sometimes ~2.5×–4.5×) | Applies when revenue is truly recurring and verified |
| SDE / profit multiple | about **2.5×–4.5×** (wider tails) | Common when the buyer is buying a job-like owner role |
| “Dev complete” / no revenue | **not** an ARR multiple | Rebuild / time-saved lens only |

Sources disagree on medians once you leave the micro tier; some blogs quote higher ARR multiples for cleaner or larger assets. **Treat any number as a prior, then underwrite the file.**

If ARR is approximately zero, stop reading multiples. You are in Type C territory.

---

## Three fractures that make “low” multiples rational

Think of the product as virtual property. The analogy helps — until it breaks.

### 1. Distribution can go to zero

Search rank, store featuring, a single API, a social channel — any of these can be repriced by a platform overnight. Brick-and-mortar rarely loses the street outside in one release note.

Buyers therefore refuse real-estate-like 20–30× rent math. They buy a **risky cash-flow stream with a kill switch**.

### 2. “Passive income” is usually a story

Unmaintained apps rot: OS changes, dependency CVEs, payment policy updates, support debt. Cash flow is often “keep paying labor so revenue does not fall.”

If the trailing profit only exists because founder labor was free, a 3× ARR sticker is already too high.

### 3. Title is scattered

Code, domain, trademark, store account, payment entity, API keys, inbox — miss one, and you did not buy the business. That transfer risk is why diligence is not optional ([checklist](due-diligence-checklist.md)).

## When a “low” multiple is still expensive

Low headline multiple can mean:

- Revenue is not durable (scraping, gray IP, one customer, spike-then-list)
- Churn is catastrophic even if trailing ARR looks fine
- Transfer will fail (personal Apple ID, non-assignable API)
- You are accidentally pricing Type C with Type A language

See also: [what not to buy](what-not-to-buy.md).

**Method (anonymous):** high traffic + tiny revenue → audit traffic quality and paywall before celebrating “upside.” Near-100% churn → do not average it away. Asking price near or below one year of *claimed* revenue → ask what the seller knows that the multiple is advertising.

(We do not publish unverified listing financials here. Case studies come later, after re-check.)

## A buyer underwriting stack

```text
1. Type A only for ARR multiples; else rebuild lens
2. Read-only revenue, refunds, concentration, trend
3. Retention / churn that matches the story
4. Transfer score (store, domain, payments, data)
5. Your 90-day fix cost (engineering + ops)
6. Then ask: does price ÷ durable profit make sense?
```

Rough mental model:

```text
Justified price ≈
  verified durable profit
  × risk-adjusted multiple
  − migration and rewrite you cannot avoid
```

If step 5 eats most of the “bargain,” there was no bargain.
